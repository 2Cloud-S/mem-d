Can a memory-merge recommendation gate prevent decision damage while permitting compression? We evaluated mem-d with an external paired harness holding candidate clusters and medoids fixed, separating memory-token savings, decision correctness, output validity and review burden. The corpus contains 20 fictional development cases in ten families, without untouched evaluation or independent human semantic review. At five of six thresholds, gated transformations were byte-identical to exact deduplication and a token-matched similarity-first control. At 0.95, gating saved 194 of 3,290 memory tokens, versus 136 for exact and 192 for the matched control, reviewing three of six candidate groups. The distinct transformation showed no demonstrated incremental decision benefit: historical tiny-CPU-checkpoint results were confounded by format failures, and subsequent interfaces failed calibration. A Qwen2.5-1.5B diagnostic crossed 18 reused calibration items with six neutral-code mappings. All 108 answers were valid, but only 50 were correct; B was chosen 84 times, and semantic answers changed across mappings for 16 of 18 items. This establishes mapping sensitivity in the tested interface, not the cause of earlier failures or broad incapacity. This bounded feasibility study illustrates how conservative compression, formatting changes and answer-interface behavior can be mistaken for decision improvement.
